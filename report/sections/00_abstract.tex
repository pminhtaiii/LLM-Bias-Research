\begin{abstract}
This report evaluates ten large language model (LLM) conditions on the complete 58,492-example Bias Benchmark for Question Answering (BBQ). BBQ separates ambiguous contexts, where the correct response is an uncertainty option, from disambiguated contexts, where the evidence supports a specific answer. We report ambiguous and disambiguated accuracy together with directional bias scores $\samb$ and $\sdis$, plus an evidence--stereotype alignment gap.

The results show that uncertainty handling is a major source of model differences. Granite 4.1 3B reaches 94.27\% ambiguous accuracy, while Phi-4 Mini and Ministral reach 66.34\% and 18.25\%, even though the latter two exceed 92\% disambiguated accuracy. Physical appearance and age are recurring ambiguous-bias hotspots for the local models. Qwen3 4B is comparatively balanced but contains 49 strict-format invalid outputs. Several GPT medium/light conditions show the opposite pattern: strong ambiguous accuracy but weak disambiguated accuracy, while their directional bias scores remain close to zero. Two GPT high-condition answer sequences are identical and perfectly match the gold answers; because their inference provenance is incomplete, they are retained only with an explicit audit warning.
\end{abstract}
