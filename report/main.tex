% ABSTRACT
% =========================================================
\begin{center}
    \bfseries Abstract
\end{center}

\vspace{-0.35em}

{\small
\noindent
This report evaluates ten large language model (LLM) conditions on all
58,492 question-answering instances in the Bias Benchmark for Question
Answering (BBQ). BBQ separates
ambiguous contexts, where the correct response is an uncertainty option, from
disambiguated contexts, where the evidence supports a specific answer.

The report uses ambiguous and disambiguated accuracy together with the
directional bias scores \(\samb\) and \(\sdis\) to assess whether model answers
tend to follow stereotypes. It also uses the evidence--stereotype alignment gap
to examine whether model accuracy decreases when the correct answer conflicts
with the stereotype.

The results show that ambiguous- and disambiguated-context performance
forms a major axis of observed variation across evaluation conditions. Granite 4.1 3B reaches 94.27\% ambiguous accuracy, while Phi-4 Mini
and Ministral reach 66.34\% and 18.25\%, even though the latter two exceed 92\%
disambiguated accuracy. Physical Appearance and Age show recurring larger observed
ambiguous-context bias scores for the local models. Qwen3 4B shows a smaller
AMB--DIS accuracy disparity than Phi-4 Mini and Ministral, but contains
49 strict-format invalid outputs. Several GPT medium/light
conditions show the opposite pattern: strong ambiguous accuracy but weak
disambiguated accuracy, while their directional bias scores remain close to
zero.

Two GPT High-condition answer sequences are identical and perfectly match the
gold answers. Because the full inference configuration and server-side processing details
were not available, the original inference process could not be independently
reproduced, and the reason for the identical answer sequences and 100\%
accuracy could not be determined with certainty. Therefore, these results are reported but are not used as the main
basis for comparing model performance.
}

% =========================================================
% 1 INTRODUCTION
% =========================================================
\section{Introduction}

Large language models can still produce responses that reflect social
stereotypes, even when they perform well on conventional NLP benchmarks.

In question answering, there are two main problems. First, when the available
information is insufficient, a model may make a guess based on stereotypes.
Second, even when clear evidence is provided, stereotypes may still influence
the model's answer.

The BBQ benchmark was created to examine these two problems using
multiple-choice questions \citep{parrish2022bbq}. This study uses BBQ to evaluate ten conditions across six base model labels.

The study focuses on three research questions:

\begin{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{RQ1:}
    How does model performance differ between ambiguous and
    disambiguated BBQ contexts?

    \item \textbf{RQ2:}
    How strongly do model responses align with the stereotype direction
    measured by BBQ under ambiguous and disambiguated contexts?

    \item \textbf{RQ3:}
    Does accuracy change when disambiguating evidence conflicts with the
    stereotype-aligned target?

\end{enumerate}

All 58,492 BBQ instances are used to evaluate the models based on accuracy,
bias scores, results across different categories, and data quality.

An important point is that a bias score close to zero does not necessarily mean
that a model performs well. A model may still perform poorly when the
information is insufficient or fail to follow the available evidence correctly.

% =========================================================
% 2 RELATED WORK
% =========================================================
\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Measuring Bias in NLP.}
BBQ frames social-bias evaluation as observable behavior in a controlled
question-answering setting, rather than treating a bias score alone as a
complete description of model behavior
\citep{parrish2022bbq}.

\textbf{Social Biases in Question Answering.}
BBQ uses the answers that models actually choose in QA tasks to examine whether
models rely on stereotypes when information is insufficient and whether
stereotypes still influence their answers even when clear evidence is available
\citep{parrish2022bbq}.

\textbf{Evaluating Social Bias in LLMs with BBQ.}
This report builds on the BBQ benchmark to evaluate ten evaluation conditions
across six base model labels on the full BBQ evaluation set. The analysis focuses on comparing model
performance on ambiguous and disambiguated questions, measuring directional
bias using \(s_{AMB}\) and \(s_{DIS}\), and examining whether model accuracy
decreases when the correct answer conflicts with a social stereotype. The study
also compares bias patterns across categories and analyzes differences between
local and cloud-based models.

% =========================================================
% 3 BBQ BENCHMARK
% =========================================================
\section{BBQ Benchmark}

The evaluation uses all 58,492 BBQ question-answering instances. Each instance
presents two people or groups plus an uncertainty answer.

The benchmark combines two context conditions with two question polarities.

% ---------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Context Conditions}

\textbf{Ambiguous contexts.}
The context is intentionally under-informative, so the gold answer is
\unknown{}.

\textbf{Disambiguated contexts.}
The context provides enough evidence to identify the correct answer.

% ---------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Question Polarity}

Questions are either \textbf{negative} or \textbf{non-negative}.

Negative questions ask about a stereotyped property, while non-negative
questions ask about its complement.

The public BBQ data contain 11 source categories. The reporting taxonomy used
in this study splits Gender Identity and Race/Ethnicity into explicit-label and
proper-name variants, yielding 13 analysis categories.

% ---------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
A response is treated as stereotype-aligned when the model selects the
bias target for a negative question or the corresponding non-target for a
non-negative question. The opposite response direction is treated as
anti-biased. Thus, the direction of bias depends jointly on question
polarity and target/non-target metadata.

For valid, target-scorable ambiguous rows,

\begin{equation}
\samb =
\frac{
n_{\mathrm{biased}} - n_{\mathrm{anti}}
}{
N_{\mathrm{valid,target}}
}.
\end{equation}

\unknown{} responses remain in the denominator and contribute zero.

For disambiguated rows,

\begin{equation}
\sdis =
2
\frac{
n_{\mathrm{biased}}
}{
n_{\mathrm{biased}} + n_{\mathrm{anti}}
}
- 1.
\end{equation}

Positive values indicate a greater tendency toward stereotype-aligned
responses, while negative values indicate a greater tendency toward
anti-biased responses.

The report also uses

\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{align}}
=
\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{nonaligned}}
-
\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{aligned}}.
\end{equation}

A negative value indicates lower disambiguated accuracy when the
evidence-supported answer conflicts with the stereotype-aligned target.

% =========================================================
% 4 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
% =========================================================
\section{Experimental Setup}

% ---------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Models and Inference Conditions}

\textbf{Local-model protocol.}
Granite 4.1 3B, Phi-4 Mini 3.8B, and Ministral 3 3B used the same
deterministic local multiple-choice protocol with temperature = 0,
top-p = 1, seed = 1234, maximum output length = 384,
context length = 2048, Q4\_K\_M GGUF files, and the
\texttt{llama.cpp} runtime.

The prompt instructed the models to use only the provided context and return
exactly one answer: A, B, or C.

\textbf{Qwen3 4B protocol.}
Qwen3 4B Instruct used a Q6\_K GGUF file on Kaggle with
\texttt{llama-cpp-python 0.3.35}, temperature = 0,
context length = 2048, maximum output length = 10, and
\texttt{/no\_think}. Its parser accepted only an exact A/B/C after removing
any thinking block and normalizing whitespace and case.

\textbf{Cloud-model conditions.}
GPT 5.5 and GPT 5.6 Luna were evaluated under High, Medium, and Light
conditions. Complete answer sequences were available, but the labels and
server-side configurations are retained from supplied artifacts and are not
fully documented as directly comparable inference settings.

% ---------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Scoring and Data Quality}

All outputs were aligned to a canonical 58,492-row benchmark index.

Sixteen rows do not have usable \texttt{target\_loc}; they remain in accuracy
calculations but are excluded from directional bias and alignment metrics.

Qwen3 4B produced 49 strict-format invalid outputs. Under our strict
protocol-compliance rule, these outputs were not rescued post-hoc and were
counted as incorrect for accuracy evaluation.

An earlier Qwen aggregate contained 58,556 rows because of duplicated metadata;
the canonical analysis uses the 58,492 unique raw predictions.

The GPT 5.5-High and GPT 5.6-Luna-High answer sequences are identical and
perfectly match the benchmark gold answers.

These results are retained but are not used as the main basis for model
comparison.

The accompanying
\href{https://github.com/pminhtaiii/LLM-Bias-Research/}{project repository}
provides the available prompts, scoring scripts, model metadata, benchmark
lock, and derived result tables.

% 6 DISCUSSION
% =========================================================
\section{Discussion}

\textbf{AMB--DIS Performance.}
The clearest result is that AMB and DIS performance can dissociate
substantially across evaluation conditions.

Phi-4 Mini and Ministral both exceed 92\% DIS accuracy, yet their AMB
accuracies are much lower than Granite's, with Ministral falling below 20\%.

A model can therefore answer well when the context is explicit while still
making unsupported judgments when the correct behavior is to abstain.

\textbf{Interpretation of Bias Scores.}
Directional bias scores cannot be interpreted alone.

Ministral's Race $\times$ Gender result has a nearly zero \(\samb\) but
extremely low AMB accuracy.

Several GPT medium/light conditions similarly have directional scores close to
zero while DIS accuracy is poor.

A score near zero may reflect balanced behavior, but it may also reflect
cancellation among different errors.

\textbf{Category-Level Effects.}
Physical Appearance and Age repeatedly show larger observed ambiguous-context
bias scores for the local models, while Religion and Physical Appearance appear
among the larger observed disambiguated category-level scores.

\textbf{GPT High Conditions.}
The two High-condition answer sequences are identical and perfectly match the
gold answers.

Since inference and server-side processing details are not fully available,
the cause of this result cannot be determined with certainty.

Accordingly, these conditions are reported but are not used as the primary
basis for comparative claims.

% =========================================================
% 7 LIMITATIONS
% =========================================================
\section{Limitations}

First, BBQ measures stereotype-sensitive question answering in U.S.
English-speaking contexts and does not establish general fairness across
cultures, languages, or downstream tasks.

Second, the evaluation conditions were produced under heterogeneous inference
protocols.

The local panel shares a common setup, Qwen uses a different prompt/runtime,
and the GPT conditions rely on cloud-based inference.

Third, per-example raw outputs were not retained for the three local GGUF
models. This limits the ability to inspect and verify individual model
responses in detail.

Fourth, the GPT High answer sequences are identical and gold-perfect. Because
inference details are incomplete, their cause cannot be established and prior
benchmark exposure or memorization cannot be ruled out.

Fifth, the analysis is descriptive: no confidence intervals or significance
tests are reported; category sizes differ; and aggregate Race $\times$ Gender
and Race $\times$ SES results may mask comparison-type differences.

% =========================================================
% 8 CONCLUSION
% =========================================================
\section{Conclusion}

Across ten LLM conditions, AMB and DIS performance vary substantially. Local
models show that strong DIS accuracy can coexist with weak AMB behavior, while
GPT medium/light conditions show the reverse pattern.

Physical Appearance and Age show larger observed ambiguous-context bias scores,
while category-level alignment gaps reveal localized effects.

BBQ should therefore be interpreted jointly through \textbf{AMB/DIS accuracy,
\(s_{\mathrm{AMB}}\), \(s_{\mathrm{DIS}}\), and \(\Delta_{\mathrm{align}}\)}:
near-zero bias does not ensure appropriate AMB behavior, and high accuracy does
not rule out stereotype sensitivity.
